    The difficulty index

    Races that pay more points per race-day attract stronger fields. The difficulty index measures how far a race's field sits from what its points would usually attract.

    Typical start list for the points paid

      Points are compared per race-day because a stage race pays its points over several days to the same field. A five-day race paying 3,000 points offers 600 a day, and is compared with other races paying 600 a day.

      Method and limits

      What is measured

      Read with care

      • A soft rating is not a free win. The softest Class 1 races are often far from Europe, with long travel and small fields. Their index is extreme because almost no one with points starts, and that can change from one year to the next.
      • Stage races include general-classification points. A team that won't contest the overall can only take stage, jersey and lesser placings, so for that team a stage race is harder than its index shows.
      • Rider strength is total season points. That favours riders who score all year, such as stage-race and all-round riders. Classics specialists look weaker than they are, which flatters cobbled one-day races such as Paris-Roubaix.
      • Terrain is not in the index. Whether a team can score depends on whether the course suits its riders. Use the index to choose between races that suit the team.
      • Points are those of the latest edition. A race moved up or down a class since then pays differently.
